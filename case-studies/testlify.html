Designing an AI-Powered Hiring Platform at Scale

Role: Product Designer | Duration: June 2023 – January 2026 (1.5 years)

testlify.com
What is Testlify?

An AI-powered assessment platform that helps companies evaluate candidates through skill tests, psychometric assessments, and conversational AI interviews. The platform includes 3,000+ pre-created tests, supports 45+ coding languages, and integrates with 100+ ATS systems.

The Challenge

Testlify operates in a high-stakes environment where every design decision directly impacts hiring outcomes. A confusing interface could cost someone a job opportunity. A poorly designed test flow could lead to candidate drop-off. An unclear reporting system could result in wrong hiring decisions.

The complexity came from three sources:

1. Multiple User Contexts

Employers ranged from solo recruiters to enterprise HR teams. Candidates varied from tech professionals to entry-level applicants. Each group had different technical literacy and expectations.

2. AI & Automation Complexity

Features like resume parsing and conversational interviews introduced new challenges: mapping AI confidence scores, handling parsing failures, and empowering manual overrides to build user trust.

3. Cascading Dependencies

Changes to question types affected test creation, candidate experience, scoring, and reports simultaneously. A single decision cascaded across multiple surfaces, requiring strict QA.

Design Principles

These principles guided every decision across 229 tickets:

1. Control Over Automation
AI should augment, not replace, human judgment. Employers always have final say over AI recommendations.
2. Transparency in Complexity
When systems make decisions (AI scoring, test logic, candidate filtering), show users exactly why and how.
3. Progressive Disclosure
Reveal complexity gradually. Show essential information first, advanced controls on demand.
4. Guardrails, Not Gates
Prevent errors through smart defaults and validation, not by blocking user actions.
5. Consistency Across Chaos
With 229 tickets spanning multiple surfaces, maintaining pattern consistency was non-negotiable.

Deep Dive: AI Resume Parser

The Ask: Build an AI-powered system that automatically screens resumes, extracts candidate data, matches qualifications, and maps candidates to workflows—reducing time-to-hire.

The Risk: AI making high-stakes decisions without oversight could lead to qualified candidates being rejected, bias creeping in, and loss of trust if the system felt like a "black box".

Design Exploration

Option 1: Fully Automatic

AI screens naturally and auto-advances candidates to assessments.

✗ Rejected: Fast, but removes human control—too risky for hiring decisions.

Option 2: Manual Review

AI extracts data; employers review every candidate before advancing.

✗ Rejected: Safe, but defeats the purpose of automation—doesn't save time.

Option 3: AI-Assisted Guarded

CHOSEN

AI parses and scores; employers set thresholds and retain manual override capabilities.

✓ Winner: Balanced automation with control; AI does heavy lifting, humans retain final authority.

Key Design Decisions

1. Confidence Score Visualization

Used a slider with snap points (50%, 70%, 90%) and color-coded zones. This made abstract scores tangible. Trade-off: Simplified continuous scores into buckets—acceptable for clear decision points.

📊 Categoric Sliders UI

2. Auto-Advance Toggle

Created an opt-in toggle for employers who want full automation vs review checkpoints. Trade-off: Added config complexity but gave critical control.

⚙️ Review Queue States

3. Rejection Handling

Designed dual paths: Auto-reject or Keep for manual review. Trade-off: Auto-reject is risky; added a high-friction warning ("Irreversible action").

🛡️ Warning Flow

4. ATS Stage Mapping

Designed a dynamic dropdown populated directly from connected ATS's actual stage names. Trade-off: Complex backend integration but zero user confusion.

🔌 ATS Synchronization Control

Validation & Impact

Validated through founder demos (recorded feedback), internal stress-testing with non-standard PDFs, and dev reviews. Expected to process 500+ resumes in minutes, significantly reducing time-to-hire.

Deep Dive: Reporting as a Product

The Problem: Employers needed to evaluate candidates quickly, but the system was fragmented: basic reports lacked depth, psychometrics weren't branded, and there was no unified export system.

Option 1: Basic Redesign

Clean up templates; keep separate PDFs.

✗ Quick fix but misses fragmentation.

Option 2: Unified Builder

Let employers customize every layout.

✗ Too complex for most users.

Option 3: Curated System

Standardized high-quality PDFs with one-click smart export.

✓ Chosen for professionalism + speed.
